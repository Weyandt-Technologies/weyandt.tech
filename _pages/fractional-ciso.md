A CISO decides what the organization does about security, what risk it's willing to accept, and answers for it when someone asks. It's a leadership job, so another piece of software doesn't fill it.

Most organizations under a few hundred people can't justify the salary. Fractional means you get the job done part time, by someone who has done the technical work underneath it.

## How this works

There are two ways to start.

A fixed-scope assessment is a defined project. I look at your environment and how you operate, then give you a written report of what I found and what to do about it, in order. Some clients take the report and handle the rest themselves.

A retainer is an agreed number of days each month. We set priorities together and revisit them each quarter. You have someone who knows your environment and can answer a question without a discovery call first.

Either way you're working with me, not a team I hand you off to.

## The first ninety days

1. **Find out what's there.** Systems, accounts, where the regulated data is, what the IT company is responsible for. This is where most of the surprises are.
2. **Rank the risks.** A short list, in order, with a rough cost next to each one.
3. **Fix the cheap serious ones.** There are always a few changes that remove a lot of risk for very little money. Those go first.
4. **Put the rest in place.** Written policies, a response plan, and a reporting rhythm.

After that it's upkeep. New systems, new vendors, new questionnaires, and re-checking the things that matter.

## Why not your IT company

Your IT company is worth keeping. But asking them whether their own work is secure puts them in a bad spot, and they make money selling you things, so they're not the ones to tell you what you don't need.

I don't sell managed IT, hardware, or licenses. If I say a control is worth paying for, that's the only reason I'm saying it.
